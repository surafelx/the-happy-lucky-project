The mentorship guide

The ways to show up, what each one asks of you, and what it can do for a child · September 2026 · first edition

Give timeSix plans, from one afternoon to a full year. Pick the one your life can actually hold.

Non-negotiableChildren’s safety first. Everyone is screened, briefed and never alone with a child.

1What a mentor is here

A mentor at Happy Lucky Chacho is not a teacher, a sponsor or a saviour. You are an older brother or sister who shows up when you said you would. Before tutoring, before any skill, the first job is to be a role model: how you care for someone, how you speak, how you keep your word. Whatever you hope to see in a child, you have to do yourself first.

The honest promise is small and real. One reliable adult, over time, changes what a child expects from the world. We would rather have you for one hour every week for a year than for a whole weekend once.

2Give time: the mentor plans

These match the choices on the sign-up form. You can change plan at any time. The only thing we ask is that you finish what you started with the children who were expecting you.

Spark one-time workshop

One afternoon on something you know well: how a phone works, how to draw a face, what a nurse does all day.

Time2–3 hours, onceScreeningID, code of conduct, briefing. Staff in the room.Helps withCuriosity and exposure. A child meets a job or an idea they did not know existed.

Monthly monthly workshop

The same group once a month, building one skill across a school term.

Time3 hours a month, for at least 3 monthsScreeningFull: ID, two referees, code of conduct, briefingHelps withA real skill with a finish line: a first program, a song, a short story, a small business plan.

Weekly weekly group mentor

You lead or co-lead a Sunday club: reading, coding, art, science or the homework hour.

Time2 hours a week, for at least one termScreeningFull, plus one Sunday shadowing another mentorHelps withSchoolwork, reading level and routine. The children know who is coming on Sunday.

Big sibling long-term mentor

You stay with the same small group for a year and know each child by name, strengths and worries.

Time2–3 hours a week, for 6 to 12 monthsScreeningFull, an interview, and a check-in with the office every 3 monthsHelps withConfidence, staying in school, and the big decisions: what to study, where to apply, what comes after the home.

Remote help remotely

For people outside Bahir Dar or abroad. Always through the institution’s staff, never one-to-one with a child.

Time1 hour a week, onlineScreeningFull. Group calls only, with staff present.Helps withEnglish practice, university and scholarship guidance, reviewing applications, career talks.

Behind the scenes materials and events

You never need to be in the room. You make the worksheets, plan the showcase, find the projector.

TimeFlexible, a few hours a monthScreeningID and code of conductHelps withEverything the mentors in the room rely on: lesson material, event days, transport, photos with consent.

6The rules everyone keeps

  • You are never alone with a child. Work happens in groups, with staff or a guardian present.
  • Only mentors aged 18 and over work directly with children.
  • No photos, names or stories of children without written consent.
  • No gifts, money or private contact (phone, social media) with a child.
  • Any worry is reported the same day to the institution and to our safeguarding lead.
  • If you must stop, tell us first. Children notice who disappears.

7How to start

Join at happyluckychacho.org and tell us which plan fits your life. If you are not sure, choose “not sure yet” and we will talk it through. Parents, guardians and elders are welcome too, as advisers and as the people who keep us honest.
